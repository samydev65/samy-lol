I finally made a personal website. I'd been meaning to for a while, and I wanted somewhere to put my projects, videos and notes that I actually own, instead of scattering everything across different apps.

## The idea

Most portfolio sites look the same: a big hero image, a few cards and a contact button. I wanted mine to feel a bit different, so I went with the look of a classic encyclopedia page, with a sidebar, an infobox, a table of contents and plain, readable articles. It's simple, it's fast and it doesn't get in the way of the content.

## The look

The design went through a few rounds. It started as an early-2000s starfield with glowing text, turned into a grey bevelled "old desktop" theme, and finally settled on the clean encyclopedia style it has now.

The profile picture is a 32×40 pixel-art character I drew pixel by pixel. Every row of pixels is a line of text in the code, and every letter is a color, so changing my shirt is literally just swapping letters.

## How it's built

- **Next.js and React** for the pages
- **Plain CSS** for the styling, with a dark mode
- **Vercel** for hosting, so every time I push to GitHub the site updates by itself
- A small **contact form** that e-mails me directly, with a once-a-day limit to keep spam out

## What I learned

Getting something online is the easy part. The real work is in the small stuff: making it look right on phones, keeping visitors' info private, handling spam and making sure things still work when a service goes down.

## What's next

I'm going to keep adding projects and videos as I make them, and use this blog to write about what I'm learning along the way. Thanks for stopping by!
